Wenyan Yang

🤖 Postdoctoral Researcher @ Robot Learning Lab, Aalto University  ·  📍 Espoo, Finland

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Aalto Robot Learning Lab, Aalto University, working with Prof. Joni Pajarinen. I study how robots can learn skills from demonstrations and from their own exploration: cross-morphology motion and demonstration transfer (retargeting one demonstration to robots with different bodies), reinforcement learning and exploration, imitation and offline reinforcement learning, and visual representation learning for manipulation.

I received my Ph.D. from Tampere University, advised by Prof. Joni-Kristian Kämäräinen. Before that I worked as a research engineer at Cargotec on learning-based autonomous control of cranes and container handling.

On-going Research

Human-to-Any-Robot Motion Retargeting: One Learning-Free Retargeter for Arbitrary Morphologies

OngoingUnpublishedResearch preview

A single learning-free retargeter maps human motion and human-object interaction (CMU mocap, OMOMO) onto robots with very different bodies. The video shows the same human clips on a quadruped with two arms (ANYmal C + two Z1), Spot with an arm, a Unitree G1 humanoid and a differential-drive base with a Lite6 arm, across locomotion, gesture and object-carrying tasks.

From Onboard Cameras to Actions: A Language-Driven Spot Agent with Semantic Scene Memory

OngoingUnpublishedResearch preview

Spot explores a kitchen with its own body and arm cameras, fuses the views into a semantic map and an object memory, and a vision-language planner calls walking, looking and grasping skills as tools to finish a spoken task (here: “pick up the donut”). Developed in simulation with Spot’s real camera rig, for transfer to the real robot.

Why Cross-Skeleton Retargeting Is Non-Identifiable: Structural Limits of Generative Motion Models

Zhiyuan Li, Wenyan Yang, Pekka Marttinen, Joni Pajarinen

NeurIPS 2026Conference

Under standard generative objectives, sparse multi-body motion data cannot pin down which source-to-target map a cross-skeleton retargeting model learns. We propose Source-Instance Fidelity (SIF) to test whether a generated motion actually follows its source clip, across humanoid robots and animation skeletons.

SUN: Reaching for Novelty in Reinforcement Learning

Wenyan Yang, Arsenii Mustafin, Dominik Baumann, Joni Pajarinen, Simone Parisi

Under reviewManuscript

Novelty alone sends an agent to goals it cannot reach, and reachability alone gives no exploration. SUN is a single goal-selection indicator, built on successor value functions, that scores goals that are both novel and reachable.
